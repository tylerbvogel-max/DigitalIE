# AI-native manufacturing program north star

## Purpose

Enable people closest to manufacturing work to create useful internal applications with AI coding agents while making the governed path faster, safer, and easier to sustain than shadow development.

The program succeeds when a domain expert can move from a real operational need to a supported application quickly, and the resulting capability remains understandable, testable, secure, observable, owned, and recoverable after its original creator moves on.

## The organizational bargain

The platform provides the difficult reusable infrastructure:

- approved repositories and development environments;
- identity, authorization, secrets, and data-access patterns;
- governed MCP portals and reusable integrations;
- agent instructions, skills, templates, and representative fixtures;
- automated quality, architecture, security, and test gates;
- deployment, telemetry, support, rollback, and retirement paths; and
- a clear promotion path from experiment to operational capability.

Creators provide the operational meaning:

- user and work or decision being improved;
- current condition, baseline, volume, delay, rework, and error cost;
- domain rules, authoritative sources, and acceptance examples;
- consequences of wrong, missing, stale, or unauthorized behavior;
- required human decision and escalation boundaries;
- an accountable operational owner; and
- evidence that the application produced the intended outcome.

## Governing principle

Make the paved road the fastest road.

Governance should be inherited through working defaults and automated evidence wherever possible. Add manual review in proportion to consequence, access, and side effect. Do not make low-risk experimentation wait for production-grade ceremony, and do not let prototype convenience silently become production authority.

## Progressive operating zones

| Zone | Permitted use | Minimum controls | Promotion evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sandbox | Synthetic-data exploration and disposable prototypes | isolated environment, no production credentials, owner, expiration | demonstrated user need and bounded use case |
| Internal utility | Read-only analysis, reporting, or local productivity | approved source, access control, tests, logging, owner/support | representative cases, source lineage, failure behavior |
| Decision support | Recommendations that influence priorities or actions | human review, error-cost analysis, stale/missing/conflicting-data tests, monitoring | evaluation thresholds, domain review, override and incident path |
| Operational workflow | Creates records, routes work, or coordinates business state | change/release control, attributable audit, rollback, service ownership | end-to-end verification and authorized release |
| Controlled execution | Narrow actions affecting governed systems or operations | least privilege, exact target/scope, idempotency, human gate where required, tripwires, revoke/rollback | explicit authority and accepted residual risk |

Model capability never promotes an application between zones. Promotion is based on the actual workflow, data, permissions, consequences, evaluation evidence, and operating controls.

## Design decision rules

1. Start with the user, workflow, decision, and measurable outcome—not the model or interface.
2. Use process change or deterministic automation when it can solve the problem reliably.
3. Keep the improvement case, source system, or controlled business record authoritative; chat and generated reports are projections.
4. Separate read, propose, approve, and execute capabilities.
5. Keep production credentials and release authority outside development agents.
6. Make wrong, missing, stale, conflicting, and malicious inputs part of the initial acceptance set.
7. Require independent review when a change has significant security, data, operational, quality, or customer consequence.
8. Provide monitoring, kill/revoke, rollback, support, and retirement before operational release.
9. Version every behavior-bearing component: code, instructions, skills, context policy, tools, dependencies, and evaluation set.
10. Convert incidents and recurring rework into fixtures, controls, reusable components, or improved platform defaults.

## What the program standardizes

- Application specification and risk/operating zone
- Repository structure and instruction hierarchy
- Identity, authorization, data lineage, and secrets
- Architecture and ownership records
- Error handling and audit events
- Unit, integration, permission, security, scenario, and interface verification
- Dependency and configuration management
- CI/CD, environment promotion, deployment, rollback, and recovery
- Structured telemetry, support ownership, and lifecycle status
- Evidence required for production release and later change

## What remains open to creators

- Operational opportunity and product concept
- User interaction and workflow design
- Domain-specific business logic within approved authority
- Choice among supported languages, frameworks, and components
- Experiment design and iteration within the sandbox
- Novel combinations of approved capabilities
- Proposed reusable components, skills, portals, and factory improvements

Standardize the safety and maintenance envelope. Do not standardize every implementation choice or force all applications into one user experience.

## Golden-path experience

A creator should be able to:

1. describe the user, workflow, outcome, data, action, and consequence;
2. receive an approved repository with shared Codex and Claude Code policy projections;
3. obtain a sandbox, synthetic fixtures, and authorized development portals;
4. build and iterate with normal agentic coding workflows;
5. receive immediate local feedback from tests and policy checks;
6. obtain a machine-generated review and release evidence packet;
7. request only the human decisions appropriate to the operating zone;
8. deploy through a standard path with monitoring and rollback attached; and
9. see usage, outcome, incidents, ownership, and lifecycle status in a common catalog.

If a creator must manually rediscover authentication, secrets, deployment, logging, testing, security, or support practices, the platform has transferred its work to the creator and weakened the paved road.

## Shadow-application posture

Do not begin with punishment. Provide a safe adoption path:

1. inventory purpose, users, data, access, actions, dependencies, and owner;
2. place the application in an operating zone based on actual consequence;
3. contain immediate credential, data, or operational risk;
4. migrate it onto the golden repository, connectors, testing, deployment, and telemetry path;
5. preserve valuable domain logic and user adoption;
6. retire duplicate or unsupported infrastructure; and
7. capture missing platform capability that made the shadow route attractive.

A shadow application is both a risk and evidence of unmet demand. Treat it as input to platform improvement.

## Measures that matter

- Time from approved opportunity to first usable sandbox
- Time from demonstrated value to governed production release
- Percentage of operational applications using the paved road
- Unsupported/orphaned application count and age
- Escaped defect, security incident, rollback, and recovery rates
- Rework caused by missing platform capabilities
- Reuse of approved components, skills, and portals
- Human override and harmful or blocked action trends
- Application ownership, support, and retirement completeness
- Measured operational benefit against baseline

Lines of code, applications generated, prompts submitted, agent tasks completed, and model benchmark scores are not program outcomes.

## North-star test

For any proposed policy, platform feature, or review step, ask:

1. Does it reduce the effort required to create a supportable application?
2. Does it improve evidence, safety, maintainability, security, or recovery in proportion to risk?
3. Can the requirement be inherited or automated instead of manually narrated?
4. Does it preserve legitimate creator choice?
5. Will it help the next owner understand and operate the application?
6. Would bypassing it create a faster path? If so, redesign the paved road.

Prefer the decision that increases trusted delivery throughput: more accepted operational value with less hidden risk, rework, and dependency on individual heroics.

## Non-goals

- Selecting a permanent model or harness vendor
- Model leaderboard or general benchmarking programs
- Central approval of every prototype or design choice
- Replacing the organization's quality, engineering, cybersecurity, or change authorities
- Allowing the platform team to become the permanent developer for every domain application
- Measuring success by AI activity rather than manufacturing outcomes
