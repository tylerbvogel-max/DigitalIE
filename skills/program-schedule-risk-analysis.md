# Program schedule and risk analysis skill

## Trigger

Use when a person asks what controls a completion date, how much schedule
flexibility exists, what a three-point duration means, or what confidence can be
placed in a schedule or cost estimate.

## Method

1. Establish the schedule revision, status date, scope, units, calendars, and
   relationship types.
2. Check completeness, logic, constraints, open ends, status integrity, and
   duration provenance before trusting calculated dates.
3. Use CPM only for the finish-to-start, zero-lag subset represented by the
   deterministic kernel. Route richer schedules to the authoritative schedule
   engine.
4. Distinguish expected-value PERT arithmetic from sampled uncertainty.
5. For simulation, require evidence for every range, a declared seed and
   iteration count, and disclosure of omitted correlation.
6. Report critical and near-critical paths, confidence percentiles, criticality
   indices, and the assumptions capable of reversing the result.
7. Prepare options for the accountable scheduler and program authority; never
   revise dates or assign contingency autonomously.

## Stop conditions

Stop interpretation when logic is incomplete, the status date is mixed,
calendars or units are unresolved, ranges have no basis, or material correlation
is omitted. Calculation can continue only as an explicitly labeled scenario.

## Outputs

Produce the input/evidence record, network diagnostics, deterministic schedule
table, risk distribution summary, assumption register, sensitivity findings,
and named human decision owner.
