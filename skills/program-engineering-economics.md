# Engineering economics skill

## Trigger

Use for capital, tooling, automation, make-or-buy, lifecycle, replacement,
break-even, payback, NPV, equivalent-annual-value, or IRR questions.

## Method

1. Define the decision, alternatives, analysis base date, common study period,
   price-year basis, and cash-flow timing.
2. Obtain the approved discount and escalation treatment; never invent them.
3. Separate relevant incremental cash consequences from unavoidable allocated
   cost. Record capacity, opportunity, transition, quality, schedule, security,
   obsolescence, residual-value, and supply-risk effects.
4. Use deterministic functions for discounting and arithmetic. Treat IRR as one
   bounded root and payback as a screening measure.
5. Run sensitivities on assumptions capable of reversing the alternative rank.
6. Reconcile units and totals to the authoritative estimate or finance source.
7. Present economics, nonfinancial tradeoffs, uncertainty, and unresolved
   evidence to the named decision authority.

## Stop conditions

Do not recommend an alternative when price bases are inconsistent, study
periods differ, approved rates are absent, relevant costs are materially
unknown, or a make-or-buy question omits producibility, quality, capacity,
technical-data, security, or supplier-risk review.

## Outputs

Produce a cash-flow table, formula receipt, lifecycle-cost comparison,
sensitivity table, break-even or crossover result where applicable, omitted-cost
register, and decision package with owner and expiration conditions.
