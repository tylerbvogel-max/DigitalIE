# Aerospace manufacturing: first 90 days learning pattern

## Days 1–30: learn the control system

Map the product families, configuration authorities, build record/traveler path, quality organization, nonconformance/MRB route, special-process footprint, production-control cadence, supplier controls, and data systems. Spend time at Gemba before designing a dashboard. Capture terms exactly as local people use them, then map them to the reference corpus.

## Days 31–60: trace one real unit and one real failure

Follow one serialized/lot-controlled article from approved definition through planning, material, operations, inspection, nonconformance if present, and release. Separately trace a recent schedule miss, shortage, or escape. Identify evidence handoffs, manual joins, rework loops, and decision wait states.

## Days 61–90: earn one narrow improvement

Choose an evidence-available problem with an authorized owner and a reversible or contained intervention. Establish baseline, demonstrate the decision path, reduce one recurring friction, and show that conformance/traceability were protected. Preserve the before/after method as a local reference case.

## Standing questions

- Where does the system depend on an expert remembering something that should be visible?
- Which records are reconstructed after the fact rather than created at the operation?
- Where does schedule pressure generate workarounds, and how is that risk detected?
- What operational information is already available but takes too long to assemble to influence the decision?
