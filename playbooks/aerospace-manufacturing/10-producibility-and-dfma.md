# Producibility and design for manufacture and assembly

## Question answered

Can the approved design be produced, assembled, inspected, tested, repaired, and sustained repeatedly at the required rate and risk?

## Review frame

Trace critical requirements and characteristics into material, process, tooling, datum, tolerance, access, joining, inspection, test, handling, preservation, and repair needs. Examine part count, unique features, orientation, fastener/tool access, tolerance interactions, standardization, error-proofing, automation suitability, process capability, supplier capability, qualification, and configuration effectivity.

Use representative product and demonstrated process data. Compare design alternatives with explicit quality, safety, performance, recurring/nonrecurring cost, schedule, capacity, maintainability, and supply-chain consequences. A lower part count or touch time is not automatically better if it concentrates risk or impairs inspection and repair.

## Integration

Run the review early and again at maturity transitions. Connect findings to requirements, design definition, PFMEA, control plan, tooling, work instructions, source selection, verification, industrialization plan, and change control. Record accepted producibility risk and decision authority.

## Stop conditions

Stop when a proposal changes form, fit, function, safety, certification basis, key characteristic, material/process specification, or approved design without authorized engineering and configuration control.

## Output

Product/configuration scope, requirement-to-process map, observed evidence, capability risks, alternative trade, calculation receipts, qualification/verification effects, recommendation, dissent, owner, and authorized disposition.
