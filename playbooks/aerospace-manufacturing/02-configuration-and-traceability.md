# Configuration, traceability, and the digital thread

## Question answered

Can we prove which approved definition applied, what was actually built, and how every material/process/inspection record connects to that unit?

## The four views

- **As-designed:** approved engineering definition, revision, effectivity, and requirements.
- **As-planned:** router/traveler, work instructions, tooling, inspection plan, and process sequence.
- **As-built:** serial/lot genealogy, actual operations, parameters, signatures, nonconformances, and test results.
- **As-maintained** where applicable: post-delivery configuration and service history.

## Diagnostic questions

- Which revision and effectivity governed this exact serial/lot at each operation?
- Can a change be traced to affected WIP, stock, tooling, work instructions, training, and supplier orders?
- Are manual transcriptions creating unverified joins in the thread?
- Can we traverse backward from a delivered unit to source material and forward from a suspect lot to every affected unit?

## Failure modes

Latest-revision-only thinking, uncontrolled local copies, missing effectivity, commingled material, lost traveler identity, and dashboards that combine records without stable keys.

## Exit

The team can state and evidence the applicable configuration and the scope of potentially affected product; unresolved identity is an escalation, not a data-cleanup task.
