# Airworthiness and certification interface

## Question answered

When does an operational/manufacturing issue require a controlled conformance, certification, or airworthiness decision—and who is authorized to make it?

## Boundary

Manufacturing produces to approved design; authorized engineering, quality, airworthiness, and delegated roles make findings or dispositions within their defined authority. The operating analyst’s job is to preserve facts, clarify scope, and route the question—not decide technical compliance.

## Trigger questions

- Does the condition affect conformity to approved design, required test evidence, or release status?
- Could it affect safety, airworthiness, customer notification, export, or a previously delivered article?
- Does it involve a design change, repair, deviation, nonconformance disposition, or delegated finding?
- Which approval holder, program/customer requirement, and authorized role govern the decision?

## Evidence packet

Product identity; governing data/effectivity; actual condition; process/test history; containment; scope assessment; relevant prior occurrences; and an explicit question for the authorized decision maker.

## Exit

The correct authority has an evidence-preserved packet and recorded decision. “Probably acceptable” is never a release basis.
