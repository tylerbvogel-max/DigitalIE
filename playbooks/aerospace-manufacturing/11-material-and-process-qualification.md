# Material and process selection and qualification

## Question answered

What evidence demonstrates that a material/process combination can repeatedly produce the required characteristics within its approved application boundary?

## Qualification frame

Identify the product configuration, requirement and criticality, material specification and condition, process specification/revision, facility and equipment, tooling, consumables, personnel qualification, parameter windows, environmental controls, coupon/article representativeness, inspection/test methods, sampling rationale, acceptance criteria, and approving authority.

Separate three claims:

1. **selection:** the candidate can plausibly meet technical and production needs;
2. **qualification:** planned evidence demonstrates capability within a declared envelope;
3. **production control:** released work remains inside that envelope with traceable records.

Use process physics and failure mechanisms to select variables and challenge extrapolation. Statistical evidence supports but does not replace applicable prescribed qualification. A capable coupon process does not automatically qualify different geometry, thickness, material lot, equipment, facility, software, cure/load path, or inspection method.

## Change and escape response

Define equivalence, requalification, deviation, and containment routes before execution. Link parameter excursions or missing records to affected serial/lot/configuration scope and authorized material review or engineering disposition.

## Output

Qualification basis, applicability envelope, evidence matrix, study design, receipts, deviations, limitations, production control/monitoring plan, requalification triggers, record package, and approvals.

Public orientation: [PRI Nadcap critical-process accreditation](https://www.p-r-i.org/nadcap/accreditation?lang=en), [IAQG certification scheme](https://iaqg.org/certification/), and [Cranfield Aerospace Manufacturing MSc](https://www.cranfield.ac.uk/courses/taught/aerospace-manufacturing). Controlled specifications and customer/program approvals govern actual qualification.
