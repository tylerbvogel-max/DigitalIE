# Quality and conformance

## Question answered

Does this product/article conform to the applicable approved definition, and is there objective evidence that it does?

## Core model

Conformance requires the correct definition, controlled process, required inspection/test, complete objective evidence, and authorized release. A part can physically look correct yet be nonconforming because its revision, material pedigree, process evidence, or required inspection status is wrong.

## Intake

Identify part/serial/lot and configuration; applicable drawing/specification and revision; process route; inspection/test status; material and special-process evidence; known deviations; customer/program requirements; and release boundary. Preserve evidence before rework, adjustment, or disposition changes it.

## Diagnostic questions

- What exact requirement is alleged to be unmet, and where is it defined?
- Is the product identity and status unmistakable at the point of work?
- Was the evidence created contemporaneously and by an authorized method?
- Is this isolated, or could sibling serials/lots/configurations share the condition?
- Does the containment protect both physical product and its digital record?

## Exit

The condition is contained, evidence-preserved, classified by authorized quality/engineering roles, and either released with objective basis or routed through the nonconformance process.
