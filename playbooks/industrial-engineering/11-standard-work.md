# Standard work and work instructions

## Question answered

What is the safest, highest-quality, repeatable known method for doing this work now—and can a person actually follow it under production conditions?

## Build from work, not a desk

Observe capable operators performing the work; include safety, quality, ergonomic, sequence, timing, material, tool/parameter, inspection, and abnormal-condition steps. Capture the why behind critical steps. Where product/configuration changes the method, make the decision point explicit.

Standard work must be visual, point-of-use, version-controlled, accessible, and specific enough to expose abnormality. Include critical steps and decision points.

## Validate

Ask a trained person unfamiliar with the draft to execute or walk through it. Check that materials, tools, information, and time are truly available. Compare first-pass yield, time, safety exposure, and workarounds before and after. Treat operator feedback as design input.

## Change discipline

Every standard has an owner, effective date, revision history, affected configurations, training/qualification requirement, and link to control/FMEA changes. If actual work departs from the standard, learn why before treating it as noncompliance.

## Exit

The standard is usable at the point of work, validated under normal conditions, and connected to training and abnormality response.
