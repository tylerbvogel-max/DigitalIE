# Change management for operational improvement

## Question answered

What must become true in the operating system for a technically sound change to be used reliably?

## Map the adoption system

Identify affected roles, work shifts, product/configuration boundaries, safety/quality approvals, training/qualification needs, systems/documents/tools, supervisors, maintainers, and measures that might unintentionally reward the old behavior.

Explain the change in operational terms: what changes at the point of work, why it matters, what stays the same, when it starts, how abnormality is handled, and where help lives. Involve the people who will carry the change before locking the design.

## Pilot before mandate when safe

Set a prediction, scope, success measure, duration/exposure, feedback route, stop condition, and decision owner. Capture workaround demand as design feedback. If a pilot is unsafe or regulated, use the required change-control path and make that constraint explicit.

## Sustain

Update standard work, training, FMEA/control plan, maintenance/documentation, and visual management together. Review adoption and outcome separately: people can comply with a change that does not improve the result, or an improvement can be fragile because it relies on heroics.

## Exit

The change has a named sponsor/owner, point-of-work readiness, a feedback-and-escalation path, effectiveness criteria, and an explicit adoption review date.
