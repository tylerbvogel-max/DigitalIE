# Human factors, ergonomics, and work measurement

## Question answered

Can people perform the required work safely, reliably, and repeatedly within the actual environment and production system?

## Study the work

Observe representative people, shifts, product variants, abnormal conditions, recovery work, personal protective equipment, tools, reach, posture, force, repetition, duration, visibility, cognitive demand, alarms, interruptions, and work-rest pattern. Protect privacy and avoid using individual performance data as a proxy for system design.

For work sampling with (n) independent representative observations and observed proportion (\hat p), the approximate standard error is:

\[
SE(\hat p)=\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}}
\]

This does not repair biased observation times, dependence, ambiguous categories, or observer effects. Stopwatch time, predetermined-time systems, ergonomic screening methods, lifting equations, exposure limits, and anthropometric data require the applicable current method and qualified interpretation.

## Analysis

Separate value-creating motion from search, reach, travel, handling, waiting, correction, and cognitive recovery. Examine the human-machine interface, error-likely situations, detectability, feedback, workload, accessibility, fatigue, skill/qualification, and consequences of deviation. Design the station and information flow before blaming compliance or adding training.

## Authority boundary

The agent may organize observations and run approved equations. It may not make medical findings, declare regulatory compliance, set exposure limits, waive a safety control, or direct a person to perform work outside qualification.

## Output

Task boundary, population and conditions observed, method/revision, evidence, exposure and work-content results, uncertainty, error-likely situations, design alternatives, safety/quality review needs, trial plan, owner, and approval.
