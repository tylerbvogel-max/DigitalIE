# Facility layout and material handling

## Question answered

How should people, equipment, material, information, inspection, and support functions be arranged to enable safe, conforming flow across expected demand and product mix?

## Evidence model

Capture from-to flows by part family and period; load size and handling unit; route, distance, frequency, queue, touch, inspection and storage status; equipment footprint and service clearance; utilities; floor loading; environmental controls; egress and restricted zones; receiving/shipping interfaces; growth scenarios; and configuration-sensitive segregation.

Use flow-distance, adjacency, center-of-gravity, space, capacity, and life-cycle-cost calculations to compare declared alternatives. Preserve units and do not combine movement counts, weight, volume, and trips without an explicit load model.

Walk each alternative using normal, peak, failure, evacuation, maintenance, quality-hold, and material-shortage scenarios. Include tooling, consumables, records, nonconforming material, hazardous material, calibration, and point-of-use information—not only production machines.

## Stop conditions

Stop when an alternative lacks safety/facilities review, violates segregation or environmental control, assumes unavailable utilities or qualified labor, displaces a queue without reducing it, or relies on unapproved building/equipment changes.

## Output

Demand/mix scenarios, current-state evidence, relationship and from-to models, space/capacity requirements, alternatives, calculation receipts, safety/quality/maintenance constraints, sensitivity, phased move and validation plan, owner, and approval.

Public orientation: [NCEES PE Industrial and Systems specification](https://ncees.org/wp-content/uploads/PE-Ind-Oct-2020_with-codes-and-standards.pdf) and [Cranfield Aerospace Manufacturing MSc](https://www.cranfield.ac.uk/courses/taught/aerospace-manufacturing).
