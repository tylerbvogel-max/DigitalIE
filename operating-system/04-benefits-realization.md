# Benefits realization

An intervention is not successful because it shipped, trained users, closed actions, or produced favorable anecdotes.

## Evaluation sequence

1. Define the decision/outcome and exposed population.
2. Establish a credible baseline and expected causal mechanism.
3. Identify concurrent changes, selection effects, and likely confounders.
4. Define leading implementation evidence and lagging outcome evidence.
5. Set success, stop, reversal, and review criteria before implementation.
6. Observe long enough to detect recurrence or displacement of risk.
7. Decide to standardize, continue testing, revise, reject, or reverse.

Report safety, quality, delivery, cost, workforce, customer, and compliance effects together. Preserve null and adverse results so the organization does not repeatedly fund attractive failures.
