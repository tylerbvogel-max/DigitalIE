# Information boundary

DigitalIE is a personal clean-room reference. Keep real operational records in employer-approved systems.

## Allowed

- Public standards orientation and publicly available methods
- Original generic playbooks, synthetic cases, and fictional data
- Sanitized lessons that cannot identify an employer, customer, program, product, person, facility, supplier, event, or controlled method
- Personal reasoning about generally applicable operating patterns

## Excluded

- Proprietary or controlled technical data, drawings, specifications, work instructions, process parameters, source code, or system exports
- Export-controlled, classified, customer-restricted, competition-sensitive, or contract-protected information
- Identifiable production, quality, supplier, employee, cost, schedule, program, or incident data
- Screenshots, copied records, meeting notes, credentials, personal information, or verbatim internal procedures
- A reconstruction detailed enough to identify the originating organization or event

## Redaction test

Before committing a workplace-derived lesson, ask:

1. Can a knowledgeable insider identify the company, program, product, supplier, site, person, or event?
2. Does it reveal actual performance, configuration, defect, schedule, cost, process capability, or system design?
3. Does it reproduce wording, fields, thresholds, identifiers, or structure from an internal record?
4. Would the lesson remain useful if every local noun, number, date, and distinctive sequence were removed?
5. Is personal retention and use permitted by employment, contract, policy, law, and professional obligations?

Any uncertain answer means stop. Keep the source in the authorized workplace system and capture only a generic question to research independently.

## Separation rule

DigitalIE may describe a reference method. It may not hold the evidence used to execute or approve actual work. Local deployment should point to employer-controlled sources by authorized identifier; this repository should never ingest those records.
